# Northwind Retail Co. — Paid Time Off (PTO) Policy

**Doc ID:** pto-policy
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026
**Owner:** People & Culture Team
**Applies To:** All full-time and part-time employees of Northwind Retail Co. (Contractors are not eligible for PTO; see Section 6.)

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how Paid Time Off (PTO) is accrued, requested, approved, and paid out at Northwind Retail Co. ("Northwind," "the Company"). PTO covers vacation, personal time, and short-term wellness days. It does not cover holidays (see the Holiday Calendar policy, doc ID `holiday-calendar`) or protected leave such as parental or medical leave (see the Leave Policy, doc ID `leave-policy`).

## 2. Eligibility and Accrual

- **Full-time employees** (30+ hours/week) accrue PTO at a rate of **1.25 days per month** (15 days/year) during their first 3 years of tenure, increasing to **1.67 days per month** (20 days/year) starting in year 4.
- **Part-time employees** (under 30 hours/week) accrue PTO on a pro-rated basis proportional to hours worked, calculated as `(hours worked / 30) * full-time accrual rate`.
- **Contractors** are not employees of Northwind and do not accrue PTO under this policy.
- Accrual begins on an employee's official hire date and is credited monthly, in arrears, on the last business day of the month.
- New hires may use accrued PTO once it has posted to their balance; there is no PTO advance for new hires.

## 3. Maximum Balance and Carryover

- Employees may carry over **up to 5 unused PTO days** into the next calendar year.
- Any unused balance above 5 days is forfeited on December 31 unless local law requires otherwise (see Section 7).
- The maximum accrued balance an employee may hold at any time is **1.5x their annual accrual rate** (e.g., 22.5 days for a 15-day/year employee). Accrual pauses once this cap is reached until the employee uses PTO to bring the balance below the cap.

## 4. Requesting PTO

1. Employees submit PTO requests through the HR system (or via the HR agent's mock ticketing tool in this course project) at least **5 business days** in advance for planned absences.
2. Requests for fewer than 5 business days' notice may still be submitted but are subject to manager discretion and business needs.
3. The employee's **direct manager must approve** the request before it is considered confirmed. Approval should occur within 2 business days of submission.
4. Employees should confirm they have a sufficient PTO balance before submitting a request. Requesting more days than the current balance allows will require using unpaid time off or borrowing against future accrual, subject to manager approval, up to a maximum of 3 negative-balance days.
5. For absences of 5 or more consecutive business days, employees should also review the Leave Policy to determine whether the absence should instead be classified as a leave of absence.

## 5. Blackout Periods and Business Needs

Certain departments (notably Retail Operations and Customer Support) may designate seasonal blackout periods (e.g., the two weeks around major holiday sales events) during which PTO requests require Director-level approval in addition to manager approval. Blackout periods are communicated by department leads at least 60 days in advance.

## 6. Contractors and Temporary Workers

Contractors engaged through staffing agencies or as independent contractors are not eligible for PTO accrual under this policy, consistent with their engagement terms. Time off for contractors is governed by their individual service agreements.

## 7. State and Local Law Variations

Where state or local law provides more generous PTO carryover, payout, or accrual protections than this policy (for example, some U.S. states treat accrued PTO as earned wages that cannot be forfeited), Northwind will comply with the applicable law, and the more generous provision applies. HR maintains a state-by-state addendum for Payroll and People & Culture staff.

## 8. Payout on Separation

Upon voluntary or involuntary separation, employees are paid out for unused, accrued PTO up to the carryover cap described in Section 3, in accordance with state law. PTO balances above the cap are not paid out.
